### DEC-030 — H6 retrieval retest: IR does not improve retrieval over BPE at toy scale (2026-08-12)

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Status:** Closed
**Scope:** H6 (retrieval/similarity)

**Decision:** H6 is closed (negative) at toy scale under the prediction-trained objective.

**Experiment:** Clean 2-epoch retest of A_v3_2ep (per-operator IR, 2254-vocab, 359.9M params,
perplexity 1.2928) vs B_small (BPE truncated to 601-vocab, 358M params), both on the same
clean 3375/376 split. This fixes the epoch confound (A_v3 was 3 epochs vs B_small 2 epochs)
and the dataset contamination (old leaky splits) that invalidated the initial H6 run.

**Results (Mode 1: eval → train, n_queryable=287, pool=3375):**
- A_v3_2ep Recall@10: 0.0052 [0.0009, 0.0113]
- B_small Recall@10: 0.0064 [0.0017, 0.0122]
- CIs overlap. A is numerically below B_small.

**Results (Mode 2: train → train, n_queryable=2641, pool=3375):**
- A_v3_2ep Recall@10: 0.0012 [0.0006, 0.0019]
- B_small Recall@10: 0.0028 [0.0016, 0.0041]
- CIs overlap. Same ordering as Mode 1.

Both modes agree: A does not beat B_small. The result is robust to the eval-split choice.

**Rationale:** The per-operator IR's explicit dependency-name tokens (gen:FullName) did not
produce more useful retrieval embeddings than BPE under next-token prediction training. This
is a strong negative: the IR was somewhat loaded in its own favor (dep names are clean tokens
in IR but surface substrings in BPE), yet it still didn't win. The epoch confound was not the
explanation — the clean comparison reproduces the null.

**What this does not settle:** Scale (H4/H8 — same 0.5B model), objective (H5 — weights are
next-token trained; a contrastive objective might differ), co-training (H9), and ATP success
(H7 — retrieval is a proxy, not a direct prover test).
